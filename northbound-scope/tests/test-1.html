Client Simulator prompt (Mode B — paste into a fresh session)

You are role-playing the owner of Han Kalguksu, a cozy, retro Korean noodle restaurant on Yonge Street in North York, on a scope call with a software studio. You are warm, not technical, and busy; English may be your second language, so you speak plainly. We do not know the real owner's name — do not invent or assert one; just speak as "the owner." Stay in character.

Rules:

  • Open with the brief above: you want an actual website (warm, a bit retro) so people can find you, see the menu, and order; right now there's only an Uber Eats page; budget is small.
  • Answer only what you're asked. Don't volunteer the operational details (that you don't own a domain, your phone number, that your photos are just phone snaps) — make the studio ask.
  • When asked what "order" means, say sending people to Uber Eats is fine — you don't want to run another system. Only if the studio probes hard about fees, admit you'd love direct ordering to avoid Uber's cut.
  • You want the site in English and Korean; if asked, yes, Chinese too would be nice (your menu already has all three).
  • People reserve by calling; you don't want an online booking system.
  • You're not technical; if asked to choose a tech stack, defer to the studio.
  • Keep entirely to the restaurant website. These preferences are assumed for practice, not the real owner's stated wishes.

What a good scope call produces — grading rubric / answer key

Pass = routes to Spark and runs a clean restaurant intake without escalating to a Build. The honest landing spot is a Spark: one warm, retro, mobile-first site — hero (story + signature dishes + a photo), a static menu (real in-store items, EN/KO and maybe ZH), hours + address + map/directions, a big click-to-call, "Order on Uber Eats / Skip / DoorDash" buttons, Instagram/Google links — deployed to a newly bought domain.

Must-catch checklist (tick each):

  • Routed to Spark — not Build, and not Launch (no heavy custom-brand project unless the owner clearly wanted one).
  • Identified the one job: be found (Google) + show menu/hours/location + drive visits and delivery-app orders. Mobile-first.
  • Resolved the "order online" ambiguity — link out to Uber Eats/Skip/DoorDash (Spark) vs. build on-site ordering + payments (a Build, not a Spark add-on). Named the escalation.
  • Treated the menu as a static page reflecting the real in-store menu/prices — not the inflated Uber Eats prices — and did not assume a live Uber Eats menu sync (that's an integration → scope). Asked who supplies the authoritative menu and how often it changes.
  • Handled languages as simple multilingual content (EN + KO, and asked about ZH given the real trilingual menu) — neither a full i18n framework nor ignored.
  • Covered domain (they likely don't own one → buy one) and the phone number for click-to-call (not on the listing → collect it).
  • Surfaced the photo gap (are there usable, rights-cleared photos? Spark = branded graphics, not a photo shoot).
  • Included restaurant essentials: hours (incl. holidays), map/directions, click-to-call, Uber Eats/social/Google links.
  • Kept the retro/cozy look Spark-light (asked for references) instead of ballooning into a custom rebrand.
  • Clarified reservations (phone vs a booking system — a system would escalate).
  • Treated stack/hosting as the studio's call; noted the owner owns the code.
  • Named the decision-maker and asked about timeline.
  • Ended with a Manifest (Spark): in-scope items, out-of-scope → future waybills, dependencies (domain purchase, menu + prices, phone, photos, logo), definition of done.
  • Bonus: offered Pulse as a future add-on for ongoing Instagram/social — the restaurant is Pulse's exact persona. (A plus, not required.)

Scoring and verdict format (for Mode A Phase 2 output and Mode B manual review):

  • Required items: 1–13. Bonus: item 14.
  • Pass threshold: ≥ 11 / 13.
  • Items 1, 3, and 13 are non-negotiable — a FAIL on any one is a FAIL overall regardless of total score. (Wrong waybill, unresolved ordering ambiguity, or no Manifest = automatic fail.)
  • Expected open items (phone number, in-store menu/prices, hard launch date, Instagram handle) must appear explicitly in the Manifest as open items or dependencies — not silently filled.

Verdict line format: VERDICT: X/13 · PASS or FAIL · [list any red flags, or "none"] BONUS: PASS or MISS

Red flags — the skill underperformed if it:

  • quoted Spark while building on-site ordering/checkout or a booking system (those are Build);
  • assumed a live Uber Eats menu sync, or used the Uber Eats prices as the site menu;
  • reached for a full i18n framework for a simple bilingual need — or ignored the language need;
  • forgot the domain purchase, the click-to-call phone number, or the photo gap;
  • over-scoped to Launch or Build with no justification from the owner's answers;
  • finished without a Manifest.
