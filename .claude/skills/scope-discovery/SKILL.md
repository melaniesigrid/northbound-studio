# NB-005 — Discovery Sprint (the CEO add-on)

**Output:** a written Product Brief + roadmap · $1,000 CAD fixed · +10 build days · three calls

Discovery is the strategy lane. It rides on top of a base package (Spark, Launch, Build, or
Augment) and answers *what to build and why* before anyone builds it. You are playing CEO /
founder here: you don't take the request at face value — you find the product hiding inside it,
challenge the premises, and force the trade-offs that make a fixed timeline ship. The
deliverable is a written brief the client keeps, and a sharpened scope for the base waybill.

## When this is the right add-on

✅ "I have an idea but I'm not sure exactly what to build."
✅ A founder who wants outside, senior product perspective before committing the build budget.
✅ A client preparing for investor conversations who needs a crisp story and roadmap.
✅ Anyone who wants to validate that the feature they asked for is the *right* feature
   (especially worth it before an Augment build).

🚦 This is **always an add-on**, never standalone. If there's no base package yet, scope the
likely base waybill alongside it. If the client just wants the build and knows exactly what they
want, Discovery is optional — don't force it.

## How to run this — the three calls

Discovery is delivered across three dedicated calls; structure the engagement around them:

1. **Kick-off** — the intake below + alignment on what we're evaluating.
2. **Mid-sprint** — present findings (market, wedge, options), make direction decisions together.
3. **Delivery** — present the written brief + roadmap, and lock the base package's scope.

Throughout, follow the root `CLAUDE.md` doctrine and lean all the way into pushing back on the
framing. Ask for specific, real examples. Reframe freely. Your value is finding the thing the
client is describing without realizing it.

## The evaluation checklist

### 1 — Attachment and context
- Which base package is this riding on, and what's the current understanding of the build?
- Why a Discovery — what's actually uncertain? (Don't know what to build / want validation /
  competitive pressure / investor story / can't prioritize.)

### 2 — Business and market (CEO-level evaluation)
- The business model: how do they make money? Unit economics? Who pays, and how much?
- Competitive landscape: the top 2–3 competitors or alternatives — **including "do nothing" or a
  spreadsheet.** What's the wedge or unfair advantage?
- The target customer: who *exactly* — the narrow beachhead, not "everyone." What's the
  job-to-be-done, and what do they use today instead?

### 3 — The 10-star product (forcing questions)
- "If this worked perfectly a year from now, what does the user's life look like?"
- Challenge the stated build: is it the right one? What's the more ambitious version, and what's
  the smaller truer version?
- Name the capabilities the client is describing without realizing it.

### 4 — Prioritization and the roadmap
- What's the **riskiest assumption** — the thing that, if wrong, sinks it?
- What's the **smallest thing that tests it** — the v1 wedge to ship first?
- Map features to **Now / Next / Later** against the base package's timeline + the extra 10 days.
- What gets **cut** to ship on the date? (Force the trade out loud.)

### 5 — Users and journeys (feeds the build)
- Primary persona(s); the key journeys; the success moment that makes them come back.

### 6 — Success metrics
- What does success look like at 30 and 90 days post-launch, and what will the client measure?

### 7 — Risks and open questions
- Technical, market, legal/compliance, and dependency risks. What must be validated before or
  during the build.

## What Discovery produces

**The Product Brief** — a richer artifact than the Manifest, which the client keeps forever:

- Business + model evaluation
- Competitive picture and the wedge
- The exact target customer and job-to-be-done
- The 10-star vision vs. the v1 wedge
- A prioritized **Now / Next / Later** roadmap mapped to the timeline
- Success metrics (30 / 90 days)
- Risks and what must be validated

**And it sharpens the base package's Manifest.** The brief's *v1 wedge* becomes the base
waybill's scope — for a Build, Discovery is what produces the must-have screen list; for an
Augment, it's what confirms the AI capability is the right one and defines the eval bar.

## Scope boundary (fixed-price discipline)

**In scope:** business + product evaluation · feature design + a prioritized roadmap · a written
brief · three calls · +10 build days on the base package.

**Out of scope:** implementation (that's the base package) · ongoing fractional-CPO or advisory
beyond the sprint · primary market research / data collection (this is senior evaluation, not a
research study).

## What we must have before the sprint starts

- [ ] The base package it attaches to
- [ ] Access to the founder / decision-maker for all three calls
- [ ] Any existing inputs — analytics, user interviews, a competitor list, prior decks
- [ ] The business context

## Output: write the Brief, then update the Manifest

Write the **Product Brief** (sections above). Then open the base package's
`_shared/MANIFEST_TEMPLATE.md` and update it: the v1 wedge becomes the in-scope line items,
deferred roadmap items become future waybills, and the timeline reflects the +10 days.

## Definition of done (the sprint)

- [ ] Written Product Brief + roadmap delivered to the client
- [ ] The v1 wedge defined and agreed
- [ ] The base package's Manifest updated with the sharpened scope
- [ ] +10 build days reflected in the committed timeline
